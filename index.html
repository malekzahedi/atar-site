The problem

Returns travel the full distance before anyone looks at them.

Fashion has the highest return rates in e-commerce. Each returned item is shipped, sorted and handled before anyone confirms what it is or what state it is in. By then most of the cost is already spent.

Late verification

Identity and condition are confirmed only at the warehouse, after transport and handling are paid for.

  • 9% of returns in the United States are fraudulent
  • Of retailers who track fraud, 65% saw more empty-box returns and 64% more decoy returns, such as counterfeits

Slow decisions

Refunds, resale and disputes wait for that late inspection, while seasonal items lose value.

  • Online returns take weeks to process, which cuts resale value for seasonal and fast-fashion items
  • Returns posted to a central warehouse take 12–16 days longer to process than returns handled in store
  • Returned fashion loses 20–50% of its value over 8–16 weeks

Cost per parcel

Every item travels alone: its own box, label, scan and pickup, then its own unpacking at the other end.

  • 40% of online shoppers buy new packaging, usually a cardboard box, just to send a return
  • In the Netherlands, 41% of return shipments are now paid for by the shopper, as webshops pass the cost on
  • Zalando alone runs around 20 dedicated return centres across Europe to unpack and sort them
19 Jul2026

Destroying returns is no longer an option.

Under the EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR), large companies may no longer destroy unsold clothing and footwear from 19 July 2026. Items returned within the withdrawal period count as unsold. Every return now needs a verified next destination.

Our solution

Inspect early, consolidate, then route.

  1. Drop-off

    Customers hand over eligible fashion items at a participating return point, without a shipping box.

  2. Guided inspection

    A trained staff member inspects the item. Artificial intelligence (AI) guides the steps and captures identity, condition and photo evidence.

  3. Consolidation

    Accepted items go into reusable transport boxes, sorted by destination. One box carries many returns.

  4. Smart routing

    Items go to the retailer, a warehouse or another agreed destination, based on the inspection result.

A person makes the inspection decision; the software supports it. Routing follows rules the retailer sets, and refunds stay under the retailer's control.

Consolidation

One reusable box for many returns, instead of one parcel per item.

Because each item is already inspected at drop-off, it can be packed with others going to the same place. Returns stop travelling as single parcels and arrive pre-sorted.
